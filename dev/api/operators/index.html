FlowGeometries.Operators.BlankMasked — Type
BlankMasked()

Write masked at a cell that is inactive or whose stencil reads an inactive cell. The default, and the only policy that never invents a value: where the stencil cannot be formed from active data, there is no derivative.

Its cost is a dead band. Every active cell within nodes - 1 of a masked cell is blanked, so a five-point derivative loses two cells either side of every coastline.

FlowGeometries.Operators.ComponentNullspace — Type
ComponentNullspace

The constants L annihilates, one per closed component: a connected component of the graph of faces of nonzero coefficient on which L·1 = 0. FlowGeometries.Operators.FaceMetrics — Type
FaceMetrics

The staggered metric of a grid under a boundary condition, built once by face_metrics and read by gradient!, divergence!, curl!, rotational_velocity!, to_faces!, to_centres! and laplacian_coefficients. Fields, each face array one per direction shaped by Grids.face_dims:

  • grid, boundary: what it describes;
  • area_op: the area through which the operators couple across a face: its geometric area between two active cells, zero against an inactive one, and at the end of a bounded direction its geometric area under Dirichlet() and zero under Neumann(); zero across a face of zero gap;
  • area_flux: the area through which data crosses a face, area_op with every bounded end open; the same array under Dirichlet();
  • gap: the physical distance between the two centres a face separates, and from its one centre to the face at the end of a bounded direction;
  • edge: per rotation pair (a, b), over its corners, the length of the primal edge through the corner, ∏ of the physical cell widths along the other directions; (nothing,) in two directions, where it is one;
  • corner: per rotation pair (a, b), over its corners, the area μ of the dual cell around the corner in the (a, b) plane, spanned by the four centres around it: on a rectilinear grid |h_a h_b Δξ_a Δξ_b| with the scale factors read at the corner and Δξ the coordinate distances between those centres;
  • volume: each cell's measure as the region its faces bound, which divergence! divides the net flux by: the grid's measure, or where a sampling's quadrature weights stand in for it, the area between the cell's faces, a pole row's included;
  • orientation: per direction σ = ±1, the sign of the coordinate's step along the index. A face value and a derivative along d are taken along increasing coordinate, whichever way an axis is stored; on a curvilinear grid, whose directions have no coordinate of their own, along increasing index.

Every area, gap, edge length and corner area is nonnegative. A gap is zero between two centres at one point, as along a row of cells on a pole, and the face there has no area: every field the grid samples has one value at that point. Under Neumann() the two areas differ at the ends of bounded directions only, where data crosses and the operators do not couple.

An operator reads it in execution's memory: one held on the host is moved there for the call, and one moved once with Execution.on_backend is read as it is.

FlowGeometries.Operators.GradientPlan — Type
GradientPlan

The geometry of a least-squares gradient, separated from any field: for each cell, the coefficient on each neighbour's difference from it, once per coordinate direction. Built by gradient_plan, applied by gradient!.

The two index buffers are typed independently and hold any Integer, so a plan built off a CSR keeps that CSR's own types.

D is the number of directions the fit resolves, which is the grid's coordinate count: two on a surface — a (λ, φ) or (x, y) mesh — and three in a volume, where the neighbourhood spans a solid angle.

apply_stencil! covers the separable case. A CurvilinearGrid has no separable axis to build a scalar stencil along, and a node set's neighbours come from connectivity, so this is the gradient for both.

The construction, with tangent-plane displacements Δrₖ from Geometry.project_to_tangent_plane, differences Δfₖ = fₖ - f₀ and weights wₖ = 1/|Δrₖ|²: minimising Σₖ wₖ (∇f·Δrₖ - Δfₖ)² gives A ∇f = b with A = Σₖ wₖ Δrₖ⊗Δrₖ and b = Σₖ wₖ Δrₖ Δfₖ. A depends only on the geometry, so it is inverted once here and the per-neighbour coefficients A⁻¹ wₖ Δrₖ are what the plan stores; each apply is then one dot product per cell and allocates nothing.

The fit is:

  • exact for a linear field on any stencil, however skewed, the least-squares combination cancelling the leading truncation term;
  • second order on locally symmetric stencils, degrading toward first on strongly skewed cells;
  • the centred difference where the stencil is separable and orthogonal (A diagonal), so it agrees with apply_stencil! where both apply.

Where A is rank deficient — a direction with no data, at a boundary or beside a mask — the pseudo-inverse zeroes that component, under the rule apply_stencil! states for a mask: a value the active data does not determine is not produced.

FlowGeometries.Operators.LaplacianCoefficients — Type
LaplacianCoefficients(grid, coef, measure)

L on a Grids.FaceIndexedGrid as data: the face coefficients coef[d][F] = A_F/g_F, one array per direction shaped by Grids.face_dims, and the cell measures, zero on an inactive cell. Its fields:

  • grid, coef, measure;
  • diag: (L e_I)_I, minus the sum of a cell's own face coefficients over its measure;
  • total: the measure of the active cells;
  • singular, nullspace: whether L has a null space, and the ComponentNullspace spanning it.

laplacian_coefficients builds it from a FaceMetrics, galerkin_coefficients from a finer level. An operator reads it in execution's memory, as it reads a FaceMetrics.

FlowGeometries.Operators.MaskedStencilPlan — Type
MaskedStencilPlan{R,I,W}

The order-th derivative weights of one direction of a masked grid, resolved per cell. row[J], over the mask's own axes, names the row of (indices, weights) cell J reads: the axis table's row J[dim] where its window holds only active cells, a row rebuilt over the cell's active run where the policy degrades it, and 0 where the cell is written masked. Rebuilt rows follow the table's n rows, each padded with zero weights to the table's width.

distance marks weights solved over the window's distances, per line, with no table rows: the form of an order ≥ 2 derivative along a direction whose own scale factor varies along it. mask is the mask the rows were resolved for; derivative! refuses a grid with another.

The apply is one gather per cell, with no mask test and no weight solve, so it runs the same on the host and in a kernel. Build one with masked_stencil_plan.

FlowGeometries.Operators.ReduceInRun — Type
ReduceInRun()

ShiftWithinRun, and where the run cannot hold nodes, use the largest window it can, down to order + 1 samples. masked below that, where no derivative of that order exists.

This trades accuracy order for coverage — a five-point scheme becomes three-point in a strait three cells wide — so it carries its own name. Ask for it when a value everywhere matters more than a uniform order.

Under this policy nodes is a ceiling, at the end of the axis as well as the end of a run: an axis with fewer than nodes samples uses as many as it has, and one with fewer than order + 1 is masked throughout. A single-latitude strip, a two-level column and a one-cell-wide channel are ordinary grids, and this asks for "second order where the axis allows it" with no clamping of nodes at the call site. The other two policies raise there, neither of them degrading.

FlowGeometries.Operators.ShiftWithinRun — Type
ShiftWithinRun()

Shift the stencil to fit inside the run of active samples containing the cell, keeping the full node count — the same thing the stencil already does at the end of a bounded axis, with the end of the active run as the boundary. masked only where the run is shorter than nodes.

The accuracy order is therefore the same everywhere a value is written, as it is under Discretization.fd_weights's inward shift at a bounded end. On a run of at least nodes active samples the weights are identical to the unmasked ones, so the interior of an active region is bit-for-bit unchanged.

FlowGeometries.Operators._bary_weights — Method
_bary_weights(grid, geo, p0, nearest, active_only) -> (a, b, c, (w₁, w₂, w₃))

The cell of the grid's Grids.CellMesh containing p0, and the barycentric weights of p0 in it. a == 0 where there is none — outside the mesh, or against an inactive node.

The cells incident on nearest are tried first, and usually hold p0. They need not: a point near an edge can lie nearer the vertex across it than any of its own. From the best of them the search walks across the edge opposite the most negative weight, which ends at the containing cell on a Delaunay mesh, or at the mesh's boundary.

The weights are computed in the tangent plane at p0, where the three vertices sit at their Geometry.local_displacements and p0 itself is the origin.

FlowGeometries.Operators._stencil_sweep_host! — Method
_stencil_sweep_host!(out, field, indices, weights, mask, masked, Val(dim), nodes, Val(N),
                     Val(M), invh, R) -> out

The host sweep. The index-parallel form serves a device launch; this one adds three things a per-work-item body cannot express:

  • iterate the Cartesian range directly, with no index recovered per cell from a linear one;
  • split the nest at dim, hoisting the stencil row — which depends only on the index along dim — out of the contiguous inner loop wherever the differenced direction is a slower-varying one;
  • carry the node count in the type, so the innermost loop has a known trip count, unrolls, and holds the weights in registers.

The arithmetic and its order are identical to _stencil_cell!, so the two paths agree bit for bit.

fac is the metric factor each value is written with — see _RowFactors — or nothing for the plain sweep. Under backend a chunk is a contiguous range of cells, written in place, so a threaded or distributed sweep is this nest over each chunk's share. dg is the degrading policy's recipe for a cell whose window reads an inactive sample, or nothing to write masked there; scratch its buffers under a serial backend.

FlowGeometries.Operators._sympinv2 — Method
_sympinv2(a, b, c, tol) -> (p11, p12, p22)

source
FlowGeometries.Operators._sympinv3 — Method
_sympinv3(A, tol) -> NTuple{3,NTuple{3,T}}

Pseudo-inverse of a symmetric positive-semidefinite 3×3.

At every rank A⁺ is a polynomial in A with no constant term, so this is closed form and needs no eigenvector. Such a polynomial annihilates the null space and acts as 1/λ on the range, which is the Moore–Penrose conditions:

  • rank 3: adj(A)/det(A), the ordinary inverse;
  • rank 2: αA² + βA with α = -(λ₁+λ₂)/(λ₁λ₂)² and β = (λ₁² + λ₁λ₂ + λ₂²)/(λ₁λ₂)²;
  • rank 1: A/λ₁², since A = λ₁vvᵀ there;
  • rank 0: zero.

FlowGeometries.Operators.apply_laplacian! — Method
apply_laplacian!(out, Φ, c::LaplacianCoefficients; execution = SerialBackend()) -> out
apply_laplacian!(f, out, Φ, c::LaplacianCoefficients; execution = SerialBackend()) -> out

out = L Φ from the coefficients, zero on every inactive cell. A face past the end of a bounded direction contributes c·(0 − Φ_I), the zero ghost of Dirichlet; under Neumann its coefficient is zero. A nonzero coefficient into an inactive cell reads Φ there, which holds zero for an operator that pins it.

With f, out[I] = f(I, (L Φ)_I) on each active cell, I its CartesianIndex: the application and the cellwise step that reads it in one pass, as a relaxation sweep or a residual takes them. out is a different array from Φ, and f writes nothing a neighbouring cell's (L Φ) reads.

FlowGeometries.Operators.apply_stencil! — Method
apply_stencil!(out, field, x, indices, weights, dim; order=1, period=nothing, mask=nothing,
               masked=zero, policy=BlankMasked(), execution=SerialBackend()) -> out

Apply a table built by Discretization.axis_stencils and keep the axis, so any mask policy works.

The table depends on the axis alone, so a caller differencing many fields along one direction builds it once. Degrading at a mask edge needs the axis to rebuild a window from, so the bare (indices, weights) form accepts only BlankMasked; this form takes both and serves every policy.

The split is the one the degrade path makes internally: the precomputed row is used wherever the window is intact, which is every cell away from a mask, and the axis is touched only where a window is rebuilt.

Building the table costs O(n · nodes²) for an axis of n samples, and holding it removes that and an O(n · nodes) allocation from every call.

FlowGeometries.Operators.apply_stencil! — Method
apply_stencil!(out, field, x, dim; order=1, nodes=order+1, period=nothing,
               mask=nothing, masked=zero) -> out
apply_stencil!(out, field, indices, weights, dim; mask=nothing, masked=zero) -> out

Apply a weight set along direction dim of field, writing out[I] = Σ_q weights[I[dim], q] · field[…, indices[I[dim], q], …].

Every convention this needs is fixed by the package: the result sits at the same location as the input, so there is no staggering decision, and the stencil shifts inward at a bounded end and wraps on a periodic one, which is Discretization.fd_weights's stated boundary behaviour and removes the need for a halo. Operations that do take those choices live elsewhere — a staggered difference, or a multi-direction operator like a divergence or a curl, each of which needs a result location and a boundary-condition policy.

Pass the axis and an order to have the weights built for you, or precomputed indices/weights from Discretization.axis_stencils to reuse them across many fields.

With a mask, a cell is written as masked when it is inactive or when its stencil reads an inactive cell — the derivative there is not determined by the active data, so it is not invented. out and field may not alias.

FlowGeometries.Operators.coarse_coefficients — Method
coarse_coefficients(fine::LaplacianCoefficients, cgrid; ghost = nothing) -> LaplacianCoefficients

L rediscretized on cgrid, a coarsen of fine.grid: a coarse face's coefficient is the coupling area of the fine faces it covers over its own gap, Σ c_f g_f / g_c with the gaps of face_metrics, and an active coarse cell's measure the sum of its children's. The mask and the boundary condition reach the coarse level through the fine coefficients. Along a direction a uniform grid halves, this is half the galerkin_coefficients there, the factor by which the Galerkin operator of piecewise-constant transfers overstates the coarse one (Braess, Computing 55 (1995) 379–393).

A fine face that couples a cell to a zero keeps the zero where it is: across it the ratio is the fine cell's distance to the zero over the coarse cell's, along the direction's coordinate. The zero is an inactive fine cell beyond an inactive coarse one, or past a bounded end the fine grid's end face, or the coordinates ghost[d] = (below, above) where a condition holds its zeros off the end faces.

FlowGeometries.Operators.coarsen — Method
coarsen(grid; pinned = false) -> grid

The next grid down: along every direction of at least four cells, coarse cell i covers fine cells 2i − 1 and 2i (cell n alone for the last of an odd count), its centre their mean; a shorter direction keeps its cells, so a grid with no direction of four cells comes back with the same cells. FlowGeometries.Operators.derivative! — Function
derivative!(out, field, grid, dim; order=1, nodes=order+1, policy=BlankMasked(),
            masked=zero, active_only=true, execution=SerialBackend(), scratch=nothing) -> out

The derivative with respect to distance along direction dim: apply_stencil! divided by the metric factor,

∂f/∂sᵈ = (1/hᵈ) · ∂f/∂ξᵈ,    hᵈ = Geometry.scale_factors(geo, p)[d]

On a Cartesian metric every hᵈ is 1 and this is apply_stencil! exactly, at no cost. Anywhere else it is the derivative a physical law is written in, singular points included.

At order = p it is the p-th derivative with respect to distance. Along a direction in Geometry.scale_invariant_directions, where hᵈ is constant along the direction, that is (1/hᵈ)ᵖ·∂ᵖf/∂ξᵈᵖ. Elsewhere — a spheroid's latitude, where h_φ = M(φ) — the weights are solved over the nodes' distances from the cell, Geometry.line_distance.

Where the metric degenerates the derivative does not exist, and masked is written. Longitude at a pole is the case: h_λ = R cos φ is zero at a latitude of ±T(π)/2, the pole as stored, so 1/h_λ does not exist there.

Each value is multiplied by (1/hᵈ)ᵖ as it is written, so a cell written masked holds exactly masked. No scale factor in this package depends on longitude, so hᵈ is constant along the first axis whichever direction is differenced, and it is solved once per direction-1 run. (It is not generally constant along the differenced direction — on a spheroid h_φ = M(φ) varies with φ — so it is not hoisted that way.)

A divergence or a curl is still the caller's to assemble, needing a result location and a boundary policy this does not choose. Note the flux form when doing so: on a sphere

∇·u = (1/(R cos φ)) [ ∂u_λ/∂λ + ∂(u_φ cos φ)/∂φ ]

so the second term differentiates u_φ cos φ, not u_φ; taking two physical derivatives and adding them is a different, wrong expression.

FlowGeometries.Operators.gradient_plan — Function
gradient_plan(grid; stencil=Stencils.Axial(1), active_only=true, conn=nothing) -> GradientPlan

Build the least-squares gradient of grid — the geometry of it, with no field involved. See GradientPlan for what it is and why it is that; apply it with gradient!.

This is the counterpart of apply_stencil! for the two architectures that have no separable axis to difference along: a CurvilinearGrid, whose neighbours come from its index topology, and an UnstructuredGrid, whose come from its stored adjacency. conn overrides the neighbour set; otherwise one is built, from stencil where the architecture takes one. A neighbour set with no edges at all raises an ArgumentError, the fit having nothing to read.

One direction per coordinate: a (λ, φ) or (x, y) surface resolves two, in its tangent plane, and a three-coordinate volume resolves three, on the local frame — see Geometry.local_displacement. The fixed-size symmetric solve behind it covers those two cases.

A masked cell gets no coefficients at all and reads zero gradient, and an inactive neighbour is not offered to the fit, on the same rule as everywhere else: not determined by the active data, so not invented.

FlowGeometries.Operators.interpolate — Function
interpolate(field, grid, p; policy=BlankMasked(), masked=NaN, …) -> value

The value of field at the coordinate p, which is how observational data arrives: a station, a float or a ship track carries a coordinate.

Discretization.interpolation_weights gives the weights along one axis; this composes them, on every layout.

  • StructuredGrid — multilinear, the tensor product of the per-axis weights. A periodic direction interpolates across its seam, so a coordinate past the last sample wraps to the first.
  • CurvilinearGrid, UnstructuredGrid — a weighted least-squares plane fitted to the k nearest cells in the tangent plane at p, which is exact for a linear field and reproduces a cell's own value at its centre. Falls back to the weighted mean where the fit is rank deficient, that being the part of it the data still determines.

Off a rectilinear grid the k nearest cells come from a ball search, and three keywords hold its state across calls. topology and scratch are as for Connectivity.neighbors_within!: with no index the search visits every cell, and Connectivity.indexed brings it to O(log n + k). nearest = (idx, dist), two vectors of at least k entries, holds the cells found. With all three held, a query allocates nothing.

p may be written any way a point is accepted elsewhere.

The mask policies say what an inactive contributor means, as they do for a stencil: BlankMasked — the default — returns masked if any contributor is inactive, and ReduceInRun renormalizes over the active ones. ShiftWithinRun has no meaning here, there being no window to shift, and says so.

A field carrying trailing batch axes beyond the grid's own — many tracers, or an ensemble, sharing one geometry — is evaluated for every element in one call: see interpolate! for the form that writes into a caller's buffer, which this one wraps.

interpolate(field, grid, points::AbstractVector; execution = SerialBackend(), …) -> values

The value at every point of points, a vector of coordinates: a vector, or for a field carrying trailing batch axes a (batch elements, points) matrix. Off a rectilinear grid the neighbour search for all of them runs on one cell list over the grid, built once, unless topology is passed.
